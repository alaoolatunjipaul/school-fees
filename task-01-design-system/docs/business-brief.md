# Business brief

## The business

Surulere Heritage Primary School is a private primary school in Surulere, 
Lagos, with about 300 pupils. Parents pay each term's fees either in full 
or in instalments, and the parent chooses which. Most of the 
conversation between school and parents currently happens by phone call, 
WhatsApp and visits to the bursar's office.

## The person who uses it

The parent is usually between 30 and 45: a trader, civil servant, 
driver, nurse or small business owner. Money is tight, so they often 
pay in parts, when they have it, and not always on the same day each 
month. They spend long hours working and commuting in Lagos, so they 
check fees in short gaps: on a bus, in a queue, at pick-up. They use a 
mid-range Android phone with data bundles that run low before the month 
ends. They are comfortable with WhatsApp and bank apps but are not 
technical, and they are wary of anything that might lose their money.

## The one thing that must work

A parent can see what they still owe this term, pay any part of it from 
their phone, and see proof straight away that the school received it.

## Three questions this school is asked repeatedly

1. "How much have I paid so far this term, and how much is left?"
2. "I paid yesterday. Has the school received it?"
3. "When is my next instalment due?"

says.)

## How the interface should feel, and why

Calm, clear and trustworthy. A parent opening a fees page is worried about 
money and wants proof a payment landed, so the amount owed and the 
confirmation must be the easiest things to see. Pages must stay light 
because data is limited, and text and numbers must be large and steady 
enough to read in a moving bus. Nothing playful or busy, because it 
would make a worried parent doubt the page.
